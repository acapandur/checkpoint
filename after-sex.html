After sex

Uncertainty needs a route, not a scandal.

The campaign names real moments without becoming explicit: a broken condom, worry about timing, testing anxiety, privacy fear or confusion about where to ask.

Safety first

This site cannot provide personal medical care. If someone may be in danger or needs urgent medical help, they should contact emergency services, a qualified health professional or a trusted local support service.

What the game teaches

Post-sex uncertainty is not one situation. Some questions are about testing. Some are about prevention. Some are about stigma. Some may be time-sensitive. A good public-health system helps people move from worry to the right professional route quickly and privately.

Calm language

Shame makes people delay. The first response should reduce panic and open a practical route.

Clear urgency

Time-sensitive concerns should point toward qualified care quickly, not toward vague reassurance.

Private entry

People should not need to trade privacy for care. Confidentiality is part of the route.

Try it as a system

The game uses fictional signals and asks you to coordinate routes through warm messages, anonymous entry, digital maps, mobile teams and professional referral pathways.
