PrEP & PEPPrEP i PEP

Prevention and urgency should not be hard to find. Prevencija i hitnost ne bi smjele biti teško dostupne.

Two abbreviations carry two of the story's three nights. Here they are in plain language — cautious on purpose, with the official sources linked underneath. Dvije kratice nose dvije od tri noći u priči. Evo ih jednostavnim jezikom – namjerno oprezno, sa službenim izvorima navedenima ispod.

Medical safety noteSigurnosna medicinska napomena

This prototype does not decide whether PrEP or PEP is right for anyone, and it does not replace qualified medical advice. Whether either applies to a specific situation is a question for professionals. Public launch copy should be reviewed by medical experts and localized to Croatian services. Ovaj prototip ne odlučuje odgovara li nekome PrEP ili PEP i ne zamjenjuje kvalificirani medicinski savjet. Odnosi li se bilo koje od toga na konkretnu situaciju, pitanje je za stručnjake. Tekst za javno pokretanje trebaju pregledati medicinski stručnjaci i prilagoditi ga hrvatskim službama.

PrEP

Pre-exposure prophylaxis: prevention before possible HIV exposure. Medicine that HIV-negative people can take so an exposure is far less likely to become an infection. It runs through a prescription, an HIV test first and regular check-ups, and it targets HIV specifically — condoms still matter for other infections. In the story, this is Ivan's night: asking about it is responsibility, not confession. Predekspozicijska profilaksa: prevencija prije moguće izloženosti HIV-u. Lijek koji HIV-negativne osobe mogu uzimati kako izloženost mnogo teže postane infekcija. Ide uz recept, prethodni HIV test i redovite kontrole te cilja specifično HIV – kondomi su i dalje važni za druge infekcije. U priči je to Ivanova noć: pitati o tome odgovornost je, a ne priznanje.

PEP

Post-exposure prophylaxis: the urgent route after a possible HIV exposure. Official sources state it should start as soon as possible and no later than 72 hours after the exposure, and it is typically a 28-day course with follow-up testing. It is prescription medicine — the route runs through emergency care and infectious-disease clinics, not a pharmacy counter. In the story, this is Ema's night: hours are the currency. Postekspozicijska profilaksa: hitna ruta nakon moguće izloženosti HIV-u. Službeni izvori navode da treba početi što prije, a najkasnije 72 sata nakon izloženosti, i obično traje 28 dana uz kontrolna testiranja. Riječ je o lijeku na recept – ruta ide preko hitne službe i klinika za infektivne bolesti, a ne preko ljekarničkog pulta. U priči je to Emina noć: sati su valuta.

The system question underneathSustavno pitanje ispod svega

The medical facts above fit on a postcard. The campaign exists because the postcard is not the problem — navigation is. At 2:47 a.m., what decides the outcome is whether a scared person can find where to go, what to say and what to ask in under five minutes, in their own language, from a source they can trust. That is what a connected CheckPoint network with clear digital navigation would change. Gornje medicinske činjenice stanu na razglednicu. Kampanja postoji zato što razglednica nije problem – problem je navigacija. U 2:47 ujutro ishod odlučuje to može li uplašena osoba u manje od pet minuta, na svom jeziku i iz izvora kojem vjeruje, saznati kamo ići, što reći i što pitati. Upravo bi to promijenila povezana mreža CheckPointa s jasnom digitalnom navigacijom.
